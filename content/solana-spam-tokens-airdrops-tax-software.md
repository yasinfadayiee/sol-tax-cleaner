# Solana spam tokens and airdrops in your tax software

Almost every active Solana wallet receives spam tokens and NFTs from strangers. Tax tools often import them as income, sometimes at an absurd value, and they bury the transactions you actually care about.

## Why it happens

Anyone can send a token to your address. The sender pays the rent for the new token account, and the tokens land in your wallet. To a tax tool that is a deposit, and it may also count the rent SOL as something you received.

## How SolTax Cleaner helps

- Incoming tokens are exported as deposits with no tag, so your tax tool doesn't treat them as rewards or income by default. You decide how to tag real airdrops.
- When someone else paid the rent for a token account opened for you, the row says so in the notes ("often a spam airdrop"), so it's easy to find.
- You can rename unknown tokens or fix tickers before you download, so real tokens are easy to tell apart from look-alike spam.

## What to do

1. [Analyse your wallet free](../#analyse) and look for deposits of tokens you don't recognise.
2. Download the CSV and import it as one custom wallet.
3. In your tax tool, ignore or hide the spam tokens, and tag real airdrops as airdrops if that's how they're treated where you live.

SolTax Cleaner doesn't calculate tax and isn't tax advice.
